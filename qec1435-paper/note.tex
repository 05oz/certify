\begin{abstract}
Whether a $\code{14}{3}{5}$ qubit stabilizer code exists is open, and this
note does not resolve it. The $[[14,3]]$ entry of Grassl's online tables
records lower bound $4$ (construction dated June 30, 2005) and upper bound
$5$ (page retrieved August 5, 2026) \cite{Grassl}, and Ball, Centelles and Huber pose
the existence question explicitly \cite{BCH}. What is proved here is a
structure theorem: if a $\code{14}{3}{5}$ exists, its group of monomial
automorphisms --- local Clifford $\times$ qubit permutation, equivalently
monomial symplectic maps on $\FF^{28}$ --- contains no element of order
divisible by $7$, $11$ or $13$, and therefore has order $2^{a}3^{b}5^{c}$.
The proof is an exhaustive, certificate-backed elimination of every
conjugacy class of monomial maps of order $7$, $11$ and $13$: three of the
four prime classes are closed by complete enumeration of the invariant
isotropic $11$-dimensional subspaces, every candidate certified to have
$d\le4$ by an exhibited low-weight vector ($1{,}298{,}700$ candidates for the fixed-point-free
order-$7$ class, $8{,}415$ for order $11$, none for order $13$); the
fourth, order $7$ with seven fixed qubits, is closed by an exact-rational
linear-programming certificate (Krawtchouk positivity: every additive
$(7,2^{9})$ code has at least $52$ nonzero vectors of symplectic weight at
most $4$) together with two short counting arguments. The composite-order
and locally twisted classes are enumerated as well, as redundant
confirmation. The corpus was re-verified adversarially with independently
written code, and the LP and branch certificates replay with the stock
Python interpreter.

The CSS case is settled by Koh et al.\ \cite{Koh26}, whose exhaustive
enumeration of CSS codes through $n=14$ shows that the maximal distance of
a CSS $[[14,3]]$ code is $4$; we cite this and claim nothing there. The
open territory for $\code{14}{3}{5}$ is thus genuinely non-CSS, with
monomial symmetries confined to $\{2,3,5\}$-groups. Negative search
data is recorded as boundary information, not as evidence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{The problem}
Let $\code{n}{k}{d}$ denote a binary (GF(4)-additive) quantum stabilizer
code on $n$ qubits with $k$ logical qubits and minimum distance $d$
\cite{Gottesman97,CRSS}. The entry $[[14,3]]$ of Grassl's online tables
\cite{Grassl} records lower bound $4$ and upper bound $5$; the
$\code{14}{3}{4}$ construction there is dated June 30, 2005 (page last
changed June 10, 2024, retrieved August 5, 2026). Both neighbors exist: $\code{15}{3}{5}$ and $\code{14}{2}{5}$ are
settled constructions in the same tables, so $\code{14}{3}{5}$ is exactly
the missing corner. Ball, Centelles and Huber raise its existence as
Research Problem~1 of \cite{BCH}. For context on nearby exhaustive work:
Bierbrauer, Fears, Marcugini and Pambianco proved $\code{13}{5}{4}$ does
not exist \cite{BFMP}; Cross and Vandeth
enumerate all general stabilizer codes through $n=9$ \cite{CrossVandeth};
and Koh et al.\ enumerate all CSS codes through $n=14$ \cite{Koh26}. On
the quantum side, automorphism groups of stabilizer codes have been
studied in their own right --- Hao \cite{Hao21} proves nonexistence
results for stabilizer codes with highly transitive automorphism groups
--- but that work contains nothing at length $14$.

\textbf{The existence of $\code{14}{3}{5}$ remains open.} This note proves
a restriction on what such a code could look like, and ships the
machine-checkable certificates behind it. It does not resolve the
question.

\subsection{The result}
Call a linear map on $\FF^{28}$ \emph{monomial} if it is a qubit
permutation composed with an invertible local symplectic map on each
qubit's $(X,Z)$-plane --- in operator language, a local Clifford circuit
times a qubit permutation, modulo Paulis; in GF(4) language, a monomial
semilinear map. These maps form the group
$\Mon \cong S_{3}\wr S_{14}$ of order $6^{14}\cdot 14!$, whose prime
divisors are exactly $2,3,5,7,11,13$. A \emph{monomial automorphism} of a
stabilizer code with stabilizer subspace $S\subset\FF^{28}$ is a monomial
map with $g(S)=S$.

\begin{theorem}[main; Theorem~\ref{thm:main} below]
No $[[14,3]]$ stabilizer code of minimum distance $\ge 5$ admits a
monomial automorphism of order $7$, $11$ or $13$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}
If a $\code{14}{3}{5}$ code exists, no element of its monomial
automorphism group has order divisible by $7$, $11$ or $13$ (Cauchy's
theorem applied to a cyclic subgroup), so the group order is of the form
$2^{a}3^{b}5^{c}$.
\end{corollary}

Two by-products are worth stating at their own strength. First, no
$[[14,3]]$ stabilizer code of \emph{any} distance has a monomial
automorphism of order $13$: the invariant-subspace dimension arithmetic is already empty
(Proposition~\ref{prop:13}). Second, every $\code{14}{3}{5}$ would be a
hyperplane (index-2 subgroup) of a $\code{14}{2}{5}$ code
(Proposition~\ref{prop:hyper}), which turns hyperplane sweeps of known
$\code{14}{2}{5}$ codes into complete searches of their neighborhoods; the
two such sweeps performed here were negative (\S\ref{sec:boundary}).

\subsection{What is not claimed}\label{ssec:notclaimed}
We do not claim that $\code{14}{3}{5}$ does not exist, and nothing here is
evidence either way about codes with trivial monomial symmetry, which one
expects to be the bulk of the search space. We do not claim the CSS case:
that is Koh et al.'s \cite{Koh26}, whose Table~VI shows the maximal
distance of a CSS $[[14,3]]$ code (pure or impure) is $4$; our own
weaker pure-CSS observation appears only as Remark~\ref{rem:css},
crediting them. We do not claim novelty for the prescribed-automorphism
technique, which is classical in coding theory (Huffman \cite{Huffman98};
see also \cite{CRSS}); what appears to be new, per the dated sweep in the front
matter, is its certified application to this open entry. The very shape
of the theorem --- pinning the automorphism group of a putative extremal
code down to a small set of orders by excluding primes class by class ---
is also classical: it is the shape of the long program on the putative
binary doubly-even self-dual $[72,36,16]$ code, opened by Conway and
Pless in 1982 and continued by many authors (the surviving group orders
there are now $1$, $3$ and $5$, by work of Borello and others); see
Huffman \cite{Huffman98} for the early history. The constructive
searches of \S\ref{sec:boundary} are boundary data, not evidence: a
rugged objective landscape says nothing about existence. Finally, the
theorem concerns \emph{monomial} automorphisms only; equivalences that
entangle qubits are not addressed.

\section{Setting}\label{sec:setting}

We use the binary symplectic picture \cite{CRSS,Gottesman97}. A vector
$v\in\FF^{28}$ is a pair $(a,b)$ of $14$-bit words ($X$- and $Z$-parts);
the symplectic form is $\omega(u,v)=|u_{a}\wedge v_{b}|+|u_{b}\wedge
v_{a}| \bmod 2$, and the symplectic weight $\wt(v)=|a\vee b|$ is the
number of qubits touched. A $[[14,3]]$ stabilizer code is an isotropic
subspace $S\subset\FF^{28}$ of dimension $11$; then
$\dim\Sperp=17$ and the minimum distance is
\[
  d \;=\; \min\{\wt(v): v\in\Sperp\setminus S\},
\]
a minimum over $2^{17}-2^{11}=129{,}024$ vectors. Monomial maps preserve
$\omega$ and $\wt$, so if $g$ is monomial and $g(S)=S'$ then $S'$ is a
code with the same parameters; in particular, closing a conjugacy class of
monomial maps for one representative closes it for all.

\begin{lemma}[prime-order normal form]\label{lem:normalform}
Let $g\in\Mon$ have prime order $p\in\{7,11,13\}$. Then $g$ is conjugate
in $\Mon$ to a pure qubit permutation of cycle type $(7,7)$ or
$(7,1^{7})$ if $p=7$, type $(11,1^{3})$ if $p=11$, and type $(13,1)$ if
$p=13$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $g=(t_{1},\dots,t_{14};\pi)$ with local parts
$t_{i}\in\mathrm{Sp}(2,\FF)\cong S_{3}$ and $\pi\in S_{14}$. Since
$S_{3}^{14}$ has no element of order $p$, $\pi\neq 1$; $\pi^{p}=1$ forces
the stated cycle types. At a fixed point $i$ of $\pi$, $g^{p}=1$ gives
$t_{i}^{p}=1$, and $\gcd(p,6)=1$ forces $t_{i}=1$. Along a $p$-cycle, the
$p$-th power acts by conjugates of the cycle product
$T=t_{i_{p}}\cdots t_{i_{1}}$, so $T=1$; and a cycle with trivial product
is conjugate, by a coordinatewise gauge $u_{j}$ chosen recursively along
the cycle, to the untwisted cycle.
\end{proof}

So the theorem reduces to four classes of pure permutations. The certified
record (\S\ref{sec:enum}--\ref{sec:order7fixed}) in fact closes more: all
monomial classes with a $14$-cycle permutation part (orders $14$, $28$,
$42$) and all locally twisted classes over the $(7,7)$ cycle type (orders
$14$, $21$, $42$; no order-$28$ class exists there, $S_{3}$ having no
element of order $4$). By Lemma~\ref{lem:normalform} these composite-order
closures are logically redundant --- a power of any such element lands in
a prime class --- and we report them as confirmation.

\section{The enumerated classes}\label{sec:enum}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
No $[[14,3]]$ stabilizer code with $d\ge5$ admits a monomial automorphism
of order $7$, $11$ or $13$.
\end{theorem}

The proof occupies this section and the next. For a fixed representative
$\sigma$, the $\sigma$-invariant isotropic $11$-dimensional subspaces are
enumerated exhaustively, and each candidate is screened by a distance
scan with early abort at weight $\le4$: the scan certifies $d\le4$ by
exhibiting a weight-$\le4$ vector of $\Sperp\setminus S$, and any
survivor would have received its exact distance by full enumeration of
$\Sperp\setminus S$ ($129{,}024$ vectors). No candidate survived.
Table~\ref{tab:classes} is the complete certified record; every nonempty
row traces to a file hashed in \S\ref{sec:artifacts} (the empty
\texttt{c13} class leaves no output file; its emptiness replays via
\texttt{gen\_generic.py c13}).

\subsection{Enumeration method}
Each representative $\sigma$ is an explicit linear map on $\FF^{28}$
preserving $\omega$. The invariant subspaces are enumerated through the
primary (Fitting) decomposition of $\FF^{28}$ as an
$\FF[\sigma]$-module --- the classical prescribed-automorphism method
\cite{Huffman98} --- with complete submodule enumeration per primary
component and isotropy imposed through the pairing of components (the
reciprocal pairing swaps the two degree-$3$ factors of $x^{7}+1$, so only
cross-orthogonality constraints survive there). Two structural examples:

\emph{Cyclic.} For the qubit $14$-cycle, $x^{14}+1=\bigl((x+1)(x^{3}+x+1)
(x^{3}+x^{2}+1)\bigr)^{2}$ over $\FF$; the invariant isotropic
$11$-dimensional subspaces number exactly $1{,}260$ ($7$ isotropic
choices in the $(x+1)$-component times $180$ orthogonal pairs in the two
cubic components).

\emph{QC-7.} For two disjoint $7$-cycles, $x^{7}+1=(x+1)pq$ is squarefree
with $p,q$ cubic; an invariant subspace is $U_{0}\oplus U_{p}\oplus
U_{q}$ with $U_{0}$ a Lagrangian plane in the $4$-dimensional fixed
component ($15$ choices) and $(U_{p},U_{q})$ an orthogonal pair of
$K$-subspaces, $K=\mathbb{F}_{8}$ ($585+42{,}705+42{,}705+585=86{,}580$
pairs), for $15\times86{,}580=1{,}298{,}700$ candidates.

Every emitted candidate is re-checked for rank $11$ and isotropy before
its distance is computed (``0 bad'' in every batch log). The batch
screener also has a positive control: run with generator count $12$ on
the reference $\code{14}{2}{5}$ matrix, it flags the code as a $d\ge5$
hit. (The control is a separate \texttt{batch 12} run --- a
$12$-generator matrix cannot ride the $11$-generator stream, where it
would be counted ``bad'' --- and its console output was not archived; it
replays in seconds.)

\subsection{Order 13}
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:13}
No $[[14,3]]$ stabilizer code, of any distance, has a monomial
automorphism of order $13$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:normalform} the automorphism may be taken to be a pure
permutation $\sigma$: a $13$-cycle plus a fixed qubit. Then the trivial
$\FF[\sigma]$-component of $\FF^{28}$ has dimension $4$ and $\Phi_{13}$
is irreducible of degree $12$ over $\FF$ (the order of $2$ mod $13$ is
$12$), so any invariant subspace has dimension $d_{0}+12m$ with
$d_{0}\le4$. No such value equals $11$. The generic enumerator confirms:
zero invariant $11$-dimensional subspaces
(Table~\ref{tab:classes}).
\end{proof}

The order-$11$ class is not empty --- the $\Phi_{11}$-part is a line over
$\mathbb{F}_{2^{10}}$, giving $33$ isotropic lines $\times\,255$ choices
of fixed-component part $=8{,}415$ candidates --- and all $8{,}415$ fail.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:normalform}, orders $11$ and $13$ are thereby fully
monomially closed.

\section{Order 7 with fixed qubits}\label{sec:order7fixed}

Let $\sigma$ be the permutation acting as a $7$-cycle on qubits
$0..6$ and fixing qubits $7..13$, and suppose $S$ is a
$\sigma$-invariant $\code{14}{3}{5}$ stabilizer subspace. Decompose
$S=U_{0}\oplus U_{p}\oplus U_{q}$ as above, now with the trivial
component of $\FF^{28}$ of dimension $16$ (the $14$ fixed-qubit
coordinates plus the two block averages), a symplectic space. Write
$d_{0}=\dim U_{0}$; then $d_{0}+3(d_{p}+d_{q})=11$, so
$d_{0}\in\{2,5,8,11\}$. Each branch is impossible:

\begin{lemma}[$d_{0}=11$]\label{lem:A}
$U_{0}$ would be an isotropic subspace of dimension $11$ in a
$16$-dimensional symplectic space, whose maximal isotropic dimension is
$8$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[LP certificate; \texttt{lemmaC\_certificate.py}]\label{lem:C}
Every additive $(7,2^{9})$ code over GF(4) --- equivalently, every
$9$-dimensional $\FF$-subspace of the $14$-dimensional symplectic space
of $7$ qubits --- has at least $52$ nonzero vectors of symplectic weight
at most $4$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Delsarte-style linear programming \cite{Delsarte} with the quaternary
Krawtchouk polynomials $K_{j}$ and the MacWilliams nonnegativity of the
trace-dual weight distribution of an additive GF(4) code \cite{CRSS}.
With $\mu=\tfrac{5}{32}$ and
$\lambda=(0,\tfrac{27}{256},0,0,0,\tfrac{1}{256},0,\tfrac{1}{384})\ge0$,
one checks exactly that
$e(w):=[w\le4]-\mu-\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}K_{j}(w)\ge0$ for $w=1,\dots,7$,
whence for any $(7,2^{9})$ additive code,
$A_{1}+\dots+A_{4}\ge 511\mu-\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}K_{j}(0)=52$.
The shipped certificate performs every step in exact rational
arithmetic (Python \texttt{fractions}); it was re-verified independently.
(An empirical floor of $104$ over $2{,}000$ random subspaces suggests the
constant is not tight; harmless.)
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Closure of $d_{0}\in\{2,5\}$]
Let $V_{\mathrm{fix}}\cong\FF^{14}$ be the span of the fixed-qubit
coordinates and let
$W=\{f\in V_{\mathrm{fix}}: f\perp_{\omega} U_{0}\}$. Since
$V_{\mathrm{fix}}$ is $\omega$-orthogonal to the block components and to
the block-average plane, $W\subseteq\Sperp$, and
$\dim W\ge 14-d_{0}\ge 9$. Moreover $W\cap S=U_{0}\cap V_{\mathrm{fix}}$
has at most $2^{d_{0}}-1\le31$ nonzero vectors, and every vector of
$W\setminus S$ lies in $\Sperp\setminus S$, hence has weight $\ge5$. But
$W$ contains a $9$-dimensional subspace supported on $7$ qubits, which by
Lemma~\ref{lem:C} has at least $52$ nonzero vectors of weight $\le4$:
contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Closure of $d_{0}=8$]
Here $d_{p}+d_{q}=1$; by the $p\leftrightarrow q$ relabeling symmetry
take $(d_{p},d_{q})=(1,0)$. The block-supported subspace
$M=V_{p}\oplus(U_{p}^{\perp}\cap V_{q})$ has dimension $9$ and lies in
$\Sperp$; its weight-$\le4$ vectors must lie in the block-only part of
$S$, contained in a subspace $B_{S}$ of dimension $\le5$ (at most $31$
nonzero vectors). Direct enumeration over all $9$ possible $K$-lines
$U_{p}$ counts $112$--$140$ nonzero weight-$\le4$ vectors in $M$
(\texttt{order7fixed\_full.txt}): contradiction. ($M$ is itself
$9$-dimensional on $7$ qubits, so Lemma~\ref{lem:C} independently forces
$\ge52$: the branch dies twice.)
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
By Lemma~\ref{lem:normalform} it suffices to treat pure permutations of
the four cycle types. Types $(7,7)$, $(11,1^{3})$ and $(13,1)$ are closed
by the exhaustive enumerations of Table~\ref{tab:classes}; type
$(7,1^{7})$ by Lemmas~\ref{lem:A} and~\ref{lem:C} with the branch
arguments above. The composite-order rows of Table~\ref{tab:classes} are
consistent redundant confirmation.
\end{proof}

\section{Verification}\label{sec:verify}

The corpus is guarded at four levels.

\emph{Controls.} The C distance checker reproduces $d=4$ on the
codetables $\code{14}{3}{4}$ matrix and $d=5$ on the codetables
$\code{14}{2}{5}$ matrix before any enumeration is trusted; batch mode
carries the positive control described in \S\ref{sec:enum}.

\emph{Independent verifier.} A standard-library-only Python verifier,
written separately, sharing no code with the search tools and using a
different algorithm (explicit construction of the full $2^{11}$-element
group and $2^{17}$-element normalizer), agrees with the C checker on the
codetables $\code{14}{3}{4}$ reference code (both find $d=4$; the
verifier reports FAIL by design, since $4<5$) and on a random sample of
$8$ enumerated cyclic candidates, with exact distance agreement in all
cases. (The verifier accepts only $11$-generator $[[14,3]]$ inputs, so
the $12$-generator $\code{14}{2}{5}$ reference is outside its scope; the
sample's console output was not archived.)

\emph{Structural cross-checks.} The cyclic class was enumerated by two
structurally different programs (CRT-idempotent construction; generic
kernel-of-matrix method): identical count ($1{,}260$) and identical
component submodule statistics. The QC-7 class likewise: the specialized
Grassmannian generator emits exactly $1{,}298{,}700$ candidates, the
generic tool independently reproduces that count (a count-only
cross-check: candidates constructed and isotropy-checked, not
re-distance-checked), and the orthogonal-pair counts match the
Gaussian-binomial predictions over $\mathbb{F}_{8}$ exactly
($585\times73=42{,}705$; $4{,}745\times9=42{,}705$).

\emph{Adversarial re-verification.} After the corpus was frozen, a
separate agent instance with independently written checker code re-ran
the verification: every enumerated candidate of every nonzero symmetry
class in Table~\ref{tab:classes} was re-checked, and Lemma~\ref{lem:C}
was replayed in exact rational arithmetic. The re-verification confirmed
every closure and also confirmed that the hyperplane and truncation
sweeps of \S\ref{sec:boundary} are complete as stated.

\section{Boundary data, and the CSS case}\label{sec:boundary}

Nothing in this section is evidence about existence; it is recorded so
that the next attempt does not repeat it.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hyper}
If $S$ is a $\code{14}{3}{5}$ stabilizer subspace, then for every logical
$l\in\Sperp\setminus S$, the subspace $T=S+\langle l\rangle$ is a
$\code{14}{2}{\ge5}$ stabilizer subspace. Hence every $\code{14}{3}{5}$
is a hyperplane (index-2 subgroup) of some $\code{14}{2}{5}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$T$ is isotropic of dimension $12$ since $l\in\Sperp$, and
$T^{\perp}\setminus T\subseteq\Sperp\setminus S$, so the minimum weight
can only go up; the tables' upper bound $d\le5$ at $[[14,2]]$
\cite{Grassl} then pins $d(T)=5$.
\end{proof}

\emph{Hyperplane sweeps.} All $4{,}095$ hyperplanes of the codetables
$\code{14}{2}{5}$ have $d\le4$; the same holds for all $4{,}095$
hyperplanes of a second $\code{14}{2}{5}$ found here by transvection
annealing (seed $44$; stabilizer in
\texttt{data/seed\_1425\_anneal44.txt}, verified $d=5$). So neither known
$\code{14}{2}{5}$ sits over a $\code{14}{3}{5}$.

\emph{Truncations.} All $45$ rank-preserving single-qubit truncations of
the codetables $\code{15}{3}{5}$ give $d<5$.

\emph{Walks and annealing.} A $d\ge5$-preserving random walk on the
$\code{14}{2}{5}$ manifold scores each visited code by its minimum, over
nonzero syndromes, of the number of weight-$\le4$ Pauli errors mapping to
that syndrome; the value $0$ anywhere would yield a candidate
$\code{14}{3}{5}$, which an exact re-check would then settle. From the
codetables seed, a
$15{,}000{,}000$-step run (seed $101$; $4{,}039{,}818$ accepted states)
drove this coverage from $13$ to $5$ within the first $251{,}000$ steps
and never below $5$ thereafter; a second seed's runner died early (step
$\approx48{,}000$ of the planned $15{,}000{,}000$) and its partial log is
retained. Random-start transvection
annealing on the $[[14,3]]$ objective floors at a single residual
weight-$4$ dual-coset vector at best (restarts typically stall at
$1$--$4$). Evidence of a rugged landscape and of a coverage
floor on the explored component, not of nonexistence.

\begin{remark}[the CSS case is Koh et al.'s]\label{rem:css}
Koh, Gong, Diaconu, Tan, Geim, Gullans, Yao, Lukin and Majidy
\cite{Koh26} exhaustively enumerate all CSS codes up to $n=14$
($2.71\times10^{10}$ inequivalent codes) with exact distances valid for
impure codes; their Table~VI at $(n,k)=(14,3)$ lists no $d\ge5$ row and
exactly two codes of distance $4$. Hence no CSS $\code{14}{3}{5}$ exists,
pure or impure, and (distances being invariant under local Cliffords)
neither does any local-Clifford image of a CSS code with these
parameters. This settles the entire CSS branch of the question by
citation, and it subsumes the only CSS statement we had derived
independently --- that a \emph{pure} CSS $\code{14}{3}{5}$ is impossible
because the best $[14,7]_{2}$ distance is exactly $4$ while
$d(C_{1})\ge5$ and $d(C_{2}^{\perp})\ge5$ would force
$\dim C_{1}\le6$ and $\dim C_{1}\ge11$ \cite{Grassl}. That two-line
argument survives only as an independent hand proof of two rows of their
computed table; the result is theirs. Their catalogue is CSS-only; the
open territory for $\code{14}{3}{5}$ is
therefore genuinely non-CSS.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[partial results toward orders 2, 3, 5]\label{rem:order5}
Outside the adversarially re-verified perimeter of
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, the working record contains a partial closure of
the order-$5$ classes by the same machinery: all order-$5$ monomial
elements reduce to pure permutations of cycle type $(5,1^{9})$ or
$(5,5,1^{4})$; the invariant dimensions satisfy $d_{0}+4e=11$; the
branches $(d_{0},e)=(11,0)$ and $(3,2)$ are impossible --- $(11,0)$ by a
weight-$2$ vector surviving in the field component, $(3,2)$ for the
one-$5$-cycle type via a second exact-rational LP certificate
(\texttt{lemmaD\_certificate.py}: every additive $(9,2^{15})$ code has at
least $1{,}471$ nonzero vectors of weight $\le4$) and for the
two-$5$-cycle type by a fixed-space dimension count --- leaving only
$(d_{0},e)=(7,1)$ open. We record this as work in progress with its
certificate, not as part of the theorem.
\end{remark}
