## Abstract

OOMMF and other finite-difference micromagnetic simulators build their demagnetizing field from the Newell demagnetization tensor of a pair of uniformly magnetized rectangular cells; some evaluate Newell's closed form, while MuMax3 computes the tensor by numerical integration. It is a textbook fact, documented but uncertified, that the closed-form evaluation of this tensor loses all significant digits by catastrophic cancellation once the two cells are more than a few hundred cell widths apart. We ask whether the tensor entries can be pinned rigorously — by two-sided rational enclosures a skeptic re-derives from a small certificate using nothing but a standard-library program — across the whole regime a real simulation uses, including the large-separation regime where double precision fails. We give such a certificate. Each entry carries an interval `[N_lo, N_hi]` of dyadic rationals with `N_lo ≤ N_true ≤ N_hi`, obtained by evaluating Newell's formulas in interval arithmetic that rounds outward on every operation; the transcendental pieces (`sqrt`, `atan`, `log`) are enclosed by rigorously-truncated series, and everything else is exact rational arithmetic. Against these enclosures we measure, rigorously, the two floating-point routes a simulator can take: the naive double-precision analytic evaluation and a truncated asymptotic expansion. The naive analytic route degrades by about six correct decimal digits per decade of separation — from ~15 digits at one cell to *zero* correct digits near 300 cells and a value with the wrong sign and wrong order of magnitude beyond — exactly the `r^6` law and the ~300-cell breakdown the accuracy literature predicts but never certified; the asymptotic route is complementary, poor at short range and good at long range, and the crossover is located rigorously. The enclosures remain tight to tens of digits everywhere, including where double precision has none. A certificate JSON and a standard-library checker are the public unit; the checker re-derives every enclosure, the naive value and every digit-loss figure from scratch, and rejects tampering both at the hash layer and at the mathematical re-derivation layer. We state what is certified, what is merely enclosed, and what is trusted, and close with the questions the table opens. A November 2026 addendum (§8) proves an explicit bound on the truncation error of the asymptotic series, valid for every rectangular cell, every tensor component and every separation larger than the cell diagonal. *(Correction, v0.17.0, 2026-11-10: through v0.16.0 the abstract called the second route "OOMMF's asymptotic expansion". The values measured are for a three-term truncation of the series; OOMMF carries a fourth term. See §4. Its first sentence also counted MuMax3, which integrates numerically, among codes using Newell's closed form.)*

## 1. The question

A finite-difference micromagnetic simulation spends almost all of its arithmetic on one thing: the demagnetizing (magnetostatic) field. In the energy-based discretization used by OOMMF and its descendants the field is a discrete convolution of the magnetization with a fixed kernel, the *demagnetization tensor* `N(r)`, whose entry `N_αβ(r)` measures the magnetostatic interaction between two uniformly magnetized rectangular cells separated by `r`. The kernel is computed once, at setup, from Newell's closed-form analytic formulas [Newell1993], and reused for the life of the simulation. Every reported number a micromagnetic study produces — a switching field, a coercivity, a resonance frequency, an energy barrier — inherits whatever error is in that kernel.

The kernel has a known pathology. Newell's formulas express each tensor entry as a fixed linear combination of a transcendental "governing function" `f` (diagonal entries) or `g` (off-diagonal) evaluated at the corners of the two cells. Each governing-function value grows like `r^3` with the separation `r`, while their combination — the tensor entry itself — decays like `1/r^3`. Evaluated in floating point, the combination is a difference of large nearly-equal quantities, and the cancellation is catastrophic. Chernyshenko and Fangohr state the consequence precisely: the relative error of the double-precision analytic formula "is of the order `10^-15 r^6`," so "for cell separations greater than `10^(15/6) ≈ 300` the analytical computation will contain no significant digits at all" [CF2015, §II.B]. The simulation packages know this and route around it: OOMMF switches to asymptotic expansions beyond a switching radius [CF2015]. Since version 2.0a2 it computes a schedule of such radii from the cell dimensions and evaluates the analytic formula in double-double (106-bit) arithmetic below the innermost one [OOMMF]; earlier releases take the analytic differences in double or long double, depending on the release and the platform. That routing is itself uncertified, and codes that do neither inherit the failure directly. *(Correction, v0.17.0, 2026-11-10: through v0.16.0 this sentence did not date the double-double evaluation.)*

This note asks a different question. Not "what is the fastest accurate kernel?" but: *can each tensor entry be bracketed rigorously, by a rational interval a stranger re-derives from a small certificate using only a standard-library program, across the entire regime a simulation uses — and can the floating-point failure then be measured, rather than merely predicted?* The cancellation that defeats double precision is not an obstruction to a certificate; it is a statement about working precision, and it is defeated outright by carrying the same arithmetic in exact rationals with outward-rounded transcendental enclosures. The output is, for each cell aspect ratio, offset direction, separation, and tensor component, a pair of dyadic rationals `N_lo ≤ N_hi` with

    N_lo ≤ N_αβ(r) ≤ N_hi,

together with the naive double value at the same point and a rigorous bracket, derived from the enclosure, on how many of its digits are correct. The comparison is the contribution: it tells a simulation author exactly where, and by how much, their tensor goes wrong.

## 2. The certified object

**The tensor.** Following [CF2015], the demagnetization tensor of two cells of common volume `|σ| = ΔxΔyΔz` with centre offset `r` is

    N(r) = (1/(4π|σ|)) ∫_σ1 ∫_σ2(r) ∇_r1 ∇_r2 (1/|r1 - r2|) dr2 dr1,         (1)

so the demagnetizing field is `H = -N·M`; in Newell's notation [Newell1993] this is `Hx = -Nxx Mx`, his formula (16). The tensor is symmetric and trace-free for `r ≠ 0`; the diagonal self-term obeys `Nxx + Nyy + Nzz = 1`. *(Correction, v0.17.0, 2026-11-10: through v0.16.0 equation (1) carried a leading minus sign, copied from [CF2015, eq. (2)]. With one gradient taken in each integration variable that sign gives the opposite of the values computed here and in OOMMF, for which two cells in line along `x` have `Nxx < 0`. The certified values were always computed from Newell's closed form with the correct sign. The `formulas.tensor_def` text of the released `demag_certificate.json` carries the same superseded sign and is left unchanged so that the record stays byte-identical.)*

**Newell's governing functions.** The double volume integral in (1) has a closed form. With `R = sqrt(x²+y²+z²)`,

    f(x,y,z) = (1/6)(2x²−y²−z²)R + (y/2)(z²−x²) asinh(y/√(x²+z²))
             + (z/2)(y²−x²) asinh(z/√(x²+y²)) − xyz·atan(yz/(xR)),          (2)

and for the off-diagonal entry,

    g(x,y,z) = −(1/3)xyR − (z³/6) atan(xy/(zR)) − (zy²/2) atan(xz/(yR))
             − (zx²/2) atan(yz/(xR)) + xyz·asinh(z/√(x²+y²))
             + (y/6)(3z²−y²) asinh(x/√(y²+z²)) + (x/6)(3z²−x²) asinh(y/√(x²+z²)),   (3)

where `asinh t = ln(t + √(1+t²))`. These are `Oxs_Newell_f` and `Oxs_Newell_g` of OOMMF's `demagcoef.cc`; we implement them in the mathematically equivalent logarithm form the code uses, `asinh(y/√(x²+z²)) = (1/2) ln((y+R)²/(x²+z²))`, and mirror every guard the code takes when an argument coordinate vanishes.

**The stencil.** The tensor entry is a second difference of the governing function in each of the three axes, at step equal to the cell edge:

    Nxx(r) = −(1/(4π ΔxΔyΔz)) · δ²x δ²y δ²z [f](r),
    Nxy(r) = −(1/(4π ΔxΔyΔz)) · δ²x δ²y δ²z [g](r),                         (4)

where `δ²x[f](x,y,z) = f(x+Δx,y,z) − 2f(x,y,z) + f(x−Δx,y,z)`, and `δ²y`, `δ²z` likewise. With its leading minus sign, (4) is a 27-point stencil with weights `−1` at the eight corners, `+2` at the twelve edges, `−4` at the six faces and `+8` at the centre, as in OOMMF's `Oxs_CalculateNxx`. The other entries are coordinate relabelings: `Nyy = Nxx` with `(x,Δx)↔(y,Δy)`, `Nzz` with `x↔z`, and `Nxz, Nyz` from `Nxy` likewise. The self-term `r=0` uses Newell's separate closed form for `Nxx(0)` (`Oxs_SelfDemagNx`), which we also carry. *(Correction, v0.17.0, 2026-11-10: through v0.16.0 equation (4) had no leading minus sign and did not define the second difference. Read with the standard second difference above, it gave the opposite of the weights stated with it. The weights, which the certified values use, are unchanged; the `formulas.stencil` text of the released `demag_certificate.json` carries the old form and is left byte-identical.)*

**The enclosure, and what is certified.** We evaluate (2)–(4) in interval arithmetic over dyadic-rational endpoints. Every interval `[a,b]` rigorously encloses the true value; every operation — addition, multiplication, division, and the three transcendentals — rounds its result *outward* to the grid of denominator `Q = 2^P` (working precision `P = 256` bits, ~77 decimal digits), which keeps the rationals bounded and makes the whole computation deterministic. The transcendentals are enclosed from below and above:

- `sqrt(q)` for nonnegative rational `q` by integer square root at grid scale: with `m = floor(sqrt(floor(qQ²)))`, `(m/Q)² ≤ q < ((m+1)/Q)²`, a two-sided rational bound;
- `atan(t)` by half-angle reduction `atan t = 2 atan(t/(1+√(1+t²)))` until `|t| ≤ 1/2`, then the alternating Maclaurin series, whose truncation error is bounded by the first omitted term;
- `ln(p)` by pulling out powers of two until the mantissa lies in `[2/3, 4/3]`, then `2 atanh((m−1)/(m+1))` with a geometric-series tail bound; `ln 2` and `π = 4(4 atan(1/5) − atan(1/239))` (Machin's formula) are certified once at the same precision. *(Correction, v0.17.0, 2026-11-10: through v0.16.0 the expression printed here was `π/4`; the `method` text of the released `demag_certificate.json` carries it unchanged.)*

What is *certified* is the two-sided inequality `N_lo ≤ N_αβ ≤ N_hi` for the analytic Newell tensor entry. What is *enclosed* rather than exact are exactly the transcendental pieces, each to rigorous rational bounds; the stencil weights, cell dimensions, offsets and all surrounding arithmetic are exact. As a numerical anchor, `demagcoef.cc` carries in its header a table of `Nxx` and `Nxy` values Donahue computed to 50 digits in Maple — 31 rows, 24 of them nonzero — and the 16 of those rows that form our anchor agree with the midpoints of our enclosures to at least 49.6 digits; containment would be the wrong test, since the enclosures are roughly 77 digits tight and a 50-digit value therefore lies outside them in general, so agreement at the gold values' own precision is what is checked. An independent 220-digit floating-point evaluation of the same formulas does lie inside every enclosure of the table. *(Erratum, v0.12.1, 2026-08-12: as first released in v0.12.0 this sentence asserted that the 16 Maple values lie inside the enclosures. That is false for the reason just given — 15 of the 16 lie outside, the exact zero being the exception — and the build log, FIXLOG.md decision S4, had already recorded the correct test. The agreement figure of 49.6 digits is unaffected, as is every certified enclosure. Only this prose claim changed. Erratum, 2026-08-12: through v0.12.1 the same sentence read "the 16 diagonal and off-diagonal values Donahue computed to 50 digits in Maple and recorded in `demagcoef.cc`", which misdescribes the source table — the header carries 31 rows, 24 of them nonzero, and 16 is the size of the subset we anchor against, not of the table. The anchor set, the agreement figure and the enclosures are unchanged.)*

**Remark (internal consistency, also certified).** The enclosures satisfy the tensor's own structural identities, and the checker can test them without any external reference. At every mutual point the interval sum `[Nxx]+[Nyy]+[Nzz]` encloses 0 (trace-freeness for `r ≠ 0`); at the self-term the same sum encloses 1; and the permutation identities hold as interval containments among the components the table carries, since `Nyy, Nzz` and `Nxz` are computed by relabeling the *same* `f` and `g` evaluations that produce `Nxx` and `Nxy` (`Nyz` is carried nowhere, so no identity involving it is tested). These are not independent physical facts to be trusted — they are consequences of (2)–(4) that a rigorous enclosure must respect, and their violation would signal a defect in the enclosure itself. Every one holds across the table.

## 3. Where this sits

The analytic formulas are Newell, Williams and Dunlop's [Newell1993]; the reference implementation is Donahue and Porter's OOMMF [OOMMF], whose analytic routines in `demagcoef.cc` we follow line for line, and whose double-double evaluation, four-term asymptotic form and model-based switching radii are the state of the practice. *(Correction, v0.17.0, 2026-11-10: through v0.16.0 this sentence did not limit "line for line" to the analytic routines. The asymptotic form used in this note was not taken from `demagcoef.cc`; see §4.)* The large-separation cancellation is analysed by Chernyshenko and Fangohr [CF2015], who give the `10^-15 r^6` error law and the `≈300`-cell breakdown, and propose numerical integration as an alternative; Bjørk and d'Aquino [Bjork2023] measure the accuracy of the analytic tensor against the dipolar field across prism, tetrahedron and cylinder tiles and report accuracy *plots* out to `10^4` tile radii for single and double precision. Both use high-precision floating point internally to obtain a reference value "exact up to machine precision" for their error estimates. None of this work produces a *certified* object: the reference values are floating-point, not two-sided rigorous enclosures; they are used inside an error study, not published as a table a third party can rely on; and there is no checker and no artifact. Our same-day novelty sweep found no certified or interval-arithmetic demagnetization-tensor table anywhere. What is new here is the intersection: a rigorous two-sided enclosure of the Newell entries across the simulation regime, a rigorous measurement of the double-precision and asymptotic failures against those enclosures, and re-verification of every entry by a standard-library checker that is not code-independent of the generator (§5). We claim that intersection, and no new tensor value or physical result within it. *(Correction, v0.17.0, 2026-11-10: through v0.16.0 this sentence called the re-verification independent; see §5.)*

## 4. Results

We instantiate the certificate over four cell aspect ratios — a cube `(1,1,1)`, a thin film `(1,1,1/4)`, an elongated cell `(1,1,4)`, and a rectangular in-plane cell `(2,1,1)` — four offset directions (along `x`, along `z`, the in-plane face diagonal, and the body diagonal), and separations from one to ten thousand cells, carrying a selected set of tensor components at each point together with the diagonal self-terms at the origin — 862 certified entries in all: 850 across fifty geometry-and-component combinations (cell, offset direction, tensor component) away from the origin, and twelve self-terms, `Nxx(0), Nyy(0), Nzz(0)` for each of the four cells. The selection is not the whole tensor at every point. For the cube, the film and the elongated cell `Nyz` is a relabelling of a component the table does carry, but for the rectangular cell on the body diagonal it is neither zero nor a relabelling — at offset `(4,2,2)` it is `≈ −8.15e−4` — so that one component, at that one cell and direction, lies outside the certified table. *(Erratum, 2026-08-12: v0.12.0 and v0.12.1 described the grid as "keeping the nonzero components at each point". **The naive analytic route, measured.** The correct-digit count of the double-precision analytic evaluation falls by almost exactly six per decade of separation — the signature of the `10^-15 r^6` relative-error law: from 15.2 digits at one cell to −8.9 at `n=10^4` is 24.1 digits over four decades, 6.0 per decade. It drops below a single correct digit near `n=300` (0.4 digits, i.e. essentially no correct significant figure, precisely the breakdown [CF2015] predicts) and below zero by `n=500`. Beyond that the value is not merely imprecise but wrong: at `n=1000` the naive `−1.14e−7` is three orders of magnitude too large, and at `n=10^4` it is `+1.17e−4` — wrong sign, nine orders of magnitude too large — while the true value is `−1.59e−13`. This is a kernel entry that a from-scratch double-precision analytic implementation returns as garbage, certified as garbage against a rigorous enclosure.

**The breakdown radius is not a single number.** The "`≈300` cells" of [CF2015] is a cube-scale rule of thumb, and the certificate shows it is exactly that. Across the four aspect ratios and four directions, the separation at which the naive double first reaches zero correct digits ranges from about 100 cells (the elongated cell, off-diagonal components) to about 2000 cells (the thin film's normal component `Nzz`, which is large and so tolerates more cancellation before the relative error reaches one). A thin film loses its *in-plane* `Nxx` near 300 cells but holds its *out-of-plane* `Nzz` to nearly 2000; an elongated cell is the reverse. The certificate records the crossing separation for each of the 50 geometry/component combinations, turning a folklore constant into a per-geometry map; Table 2 shows a representative slice.

**The asymptotic route, and the crossover.** A truncated asymptotic expansion is the mirror image of the analytic route. It is poor at short range (0.5 correct digits at `n=1`, where the `1/r` expansion has no right to converge) and good at long range; in Table 1 it reaches its double-precision floor, near 16 digits, from about 100 cells for four terms and about 300 for three, and for other cells and directions it can take longer (for the film `(1,1,1/4)` along its normal, about 500 cells for four terms and 2,000 for three). The two floating-point routes therefore cross. For the cube on-axis `Nxx` and the three-term form, the naive analytic value is the more accurate up to about thirteen cells and the asymptotic from about twenty-one on (8.6 versus 7.7 digits at `n=13`; 7.1 versus 9.0 at `n=21`). The four-term form crosses earlier: 9.6 versus 7.3 digits at `n=8`, 8.6 versus 9.0 at `n=13`. Both are dominated everywhere by the certified enclosure, and because the enclosure brackets the truth, the crossover radius of a given truncation is itself bracketed rigorously by the certificate, per aspect ratio and direction. These crossovers describe a code that evaluates both routes in double precision. **The enclosures stay tight where double precision has nothing.** The point of carrying exact rationals is that the cancellation is defeated, not merely diagnosed. At the canonical far point, the cube on-axis `Nxx` at `n=10^4`, the certified enclosure has width `≈3.6e−67` about a value of magnitude `1.6e−13` — a relative width near `2e−54`, i.e. the entry is pinned to some fifty-four correct digits exactly where the double-precision analytic formula has none. Across the whole table the relative enclosure width never exceeds `1.7e−46`, attained at the elongated cell on the body diagonal, still some forty-five correct digits. We had pre-registered a kill condition: if the certified enclosures could not be made tighter than double precision anywhere in the regime of interest, the target dies. They are tighter than double precision *everywhere* in the regime, and by tens of digits where it matters most. The target is **LIVE**.

**Self-demag factors.** As a byproduct the certificate carries the diagonal self-terms `r=0` — the single most-used demag numbers, the thin-film and cube demagnetizing factors — as certified rationals: the cube's `Nxx(0)=1/3` exactly, and the film and rod factors to the full working precision. These have no cancellation pathology; we include them because a certified reference table of demag constants is exactly the kind of common, load-bearing, unowned object this program targets.

## 5. What is certified, what is enclosed, what is trusted

**Machine-checked, replayable with standard-library Python.** The checker reads only the certificate. It (i) recomputes the SHA-256 of the canonical entry list; (ii) for every entry, recomputes the enclosure by interval arithmetic and Newell evaluation at the certificate's stated precision — code that is not independent of the private engine; see below — and verifies that the recomputed rigorous interval lies inside the certified `[N_lo, N_hi]` — which pins `N_lo ≤ N_true ≤ N_hi` — while rejecting an enclosure that has been narrowed off the truth, shifted, or implausibly widened; (iii) recomputes the naive double value and requires agreement to a relative `10^-4` (bit for bit with `--strict-naive`; the low bits of a naive value depend on the platform's `libm`), and recomputes each rigorous digit-loss bracket from the recorded value. *(Correction, v0.17.0, 2026-11-10: through v0.16.0 clause (iii) said bit for bit; since v0.16.0 that is the `--strict-naive` mode.)* Any mismatch prints `CHECK FAIL` and exits nonzero. The checker imports only `sys`, `json`, `math`, `hashlib`, `fractions` and `collections`; it uses no signals, subprocesses, network or wall-clock, and runs unchanged under a clean environment. *(Erratum, 2026-08-12: v0.12.0 and v0.12.1 gave this import list without `collections`, which `check_demag.py` imports for the consistency-identity pass. The list is now complete; every import remains in the Python standard library, and nothing about the checker changed.)* A tamper battery — an enclosure narrowed to exclude the true value, an enclosure shifted off it, an enclosure grossly widened, a corrupted naive value, an altered digit-loss claim, a falsified hash — is rejected nonzero in every case, at the hash layer and, when the hash is recomputed to match, at the mathematical re-derivation layer.

**Trusted.** Three things.

1. *The Newell formulas are the object.* The certificate bounds the value of the analytic Newell tensor entry (2)–(4). Whether that analytic entry is the right physical kernel for a given discretization — the energy-based averaging, the rectangular-cell assumption — is Newell's modelling choice [Newell1993], not something a certificate can establish. We bound the number the simulators actually compute.
2. *CPython, the operating system, and the hardware*, for the checker's own exact-rational arithmetic.
3. *SHA-256 collision resistance*, for the bookkeeping only; a skeptic can regenerate and re-check every enclosure without trusting any hash.

The generator that emitted the certificate is not trusted for any value: the checker recomputes every enclosure from the certificate's own parameters and rejects any entry it cannot reproduce. It is not, however, code-independent of the private files that built the certificate. Counted on the v0.15.0 file, 212 of the 558 executable lines of `check_demag.py` appear verbatim in `gen_demag.py`, `civ.py`, `newell.py` and `validate.py`. The naive-double Newell block, lines 393 to 509 of the shipped file, shares 80 of its 103 lines with those files, 76 of them with `newell.py`, and contains byte-identical runs of 31 and 26 lines against `newell.py`; the interval Newell evaluation shares 43 of 143 lines and the certified interval class 56 of 152 with the same files; the digit-loss bracket shares 10 of 28 with `gen_demag.py`. A passing check may therefore not be read, on its own, as an independent re-derivation of Newell's formulas, of the interval arithmetic, or of the double-precision pathology. *(Correction, v0.17.0, 2026-11-10: the counts in this section were measured on the v0.15.0 file. The v0.16.0 portability change brought it to 568 executable lines, and the line numbers, which through v0.16.0 were those of v0.15.0, are now those of the shipped file.)*

Two things stand outside that shared lineage, and they are what the result rests on. The first is external. `demag-scripts/anchor_check.py` takes 16 rows of the 31-row Maple 50-digit check table published in OOMMF's `demagcoef.cc` — a gold standard computed by other people, in other software, for other purposes — and requires the checker's own recomputed enclosure midpoint to agree with each to at least 48 digits. That anchors 16 rows of 862. It is a spot check against an external standard, not coverage of the table, and the remaining 846 entries have no external anchor. The second is internal and covers every entry. At lines 663 to 691 the checker imposes two consistency identities on the certified endpoints themselves, with no re-derivation involved: the three diagonal components must sum to an interval enclosing 0 at each of the 136 mutual points, and enclosing 1 at each of the 4 self-terms. Apart from a bare `else:`, those lines share no text with the private files, and neither do the comparisons against the certificate or the fail-and-exit reporting.

One part of the bookkeeping is shared, necessarily. The canonical serialisation of an entry and the SHA-256 recomputation, lines 545 to 549 and 580 to 581, are common with `gen_demag.py`: both sides must serialise the entry list identically or no hash could ever agree. The hash check therefore establishes that the shipped entry list is the one that was hashed, not that two independent implementations agree on how to hash it. The tamper battery rejects at the hash layer and, when the hash is made to match, at the mathematical layer; both remain in force.

## 6. The reference table, and certification

The permanent record is a single certificate JSON, holding the method parameters (working precision, transcendental enclosure scheme, stencil) and, for each grid point, the certified enclosure, the naive double value, the value of the three-term asymptotic truncation where defined (stored under the field name `asymp_oommf`, which predates the correction in §4; the certificate's `formulas` and `method` texts likewise predate the corrections in §2 and §4), and the rigorous digit-loss brackets. With it goes the standard-library checker, which re-derives every enclosure, naive value and digit-loss bracket. The checker does not recompute the stored asymptotic value. `demag-scripts/four_term_digits.py` recomputes the three-term truncation from the series (6) and prints its correct digits; it does not read the stored value. The two together are the public unit; a reader with nothing but CPython replays any enclosure from the certificate alone. The novelty sweep (dated, shipped with the artifacts) found no certified or interval-arithmetic demagnetization-tensor table in the literature: the accuracy studies [CF2015, Bjork2023] use floating-point reference values inside error plots, and the newer codes inherit the same uncertified kernels. The generator and the search that chose the grid remain private, per this program's boundary; they are not needed to check anything. *(Correction, v0.17.0, 2026-11-10: through v0.16.0 this paragraph said the checker re-derives all of the record, the asymptotic value included.)*

## 7. Questions

**Question 7.1 (The certified crossover, shipped to the codes).** The certificate brackets, per aspect ratio and direction, the separation at which a truncated asymptotic expansion overtakes the naive analytic formula. Can this crossover map be turned into a certified switching rule that a simulator adopts directly — a table of "use analytic below `n*`, asymptotic above" with `n*` rigorously bracketed — and does the double-double arithmetic OOMMF uses below the cutoff move `n*` by exactly the amount the `10^-15 → 10^-32` precision change predicts? OOMMF already sets its switching radii from assumed error and speed models (§8); the question is whether radii with certified error bounds can be supplied to such a schedule. *(Correction, v0.17.0, 2026-11-10: "OOMMF's asymptotic expansion" replaced; see §4.)*

**Question 7.2 (Certifying the asymptotic tail).** The asymptotic expansion is itself only enclosed here through its floating-point value. Can its truncation error be enclosed rigorously, by a certified remainder bound, so that, together with the analytic enclosure, one obtains a single certified kernel that is tight at *every* separation, short and long, with a proof-carrying handover in between? *(Partly answered in §8, v0.17.0, 2026-11-10: an explicit bound on the truncation error for every rectangular cell, tensor component and separation larger than the cell diagonal. The single certified kernel with a proof-carrying handover remains open. Through v0.16.0 this question spoke of "OOMMF's order-`1/r^6` expansion"; see §4.)*

**Question 7.3 (Beyond the rectangular prism).** The tetrahedral and cylindrical tiles of [Bjork2023], and the periodic and spatially-adaptive kernels of the newer codes, share the same cancellation structure. Do their governing functions admit the same outward-rounded interval treatment, and is the breakdown radius a computable function of the tile geometry?

## 8. Addendum (v0.17.0, November 2026): a bound on the asymptotic truncation error

**The series.** Take two identical cells with edges `(Δx, Δy, Δz)` parallel to the axes, volume `V = ΔxΔyΔz` (written `|σ|` in (1)) and centre offset `r`, with length `r = |r|` and direction `r̂`, and let `D = sqrt(Δx² + Δy² + Δz²)` be the cell diagonal. In this section `n` denotes a truncation order; in §4 it counts cells. Let `u` be the difference of two independent uniform points of the cell. Each coordinate of `u`, for example `u_x`, has the triangular law on `[−Δx, Δx]`, so `E[u_x^(2m)] = μ_m Δx^(2m)` with `μ_m = 2/((2m+1)(2m+2))`, and likewise for `y` and `z`. For cells that do not overlap, (1) is

    N_ij(r) = −(V/4π) E[ ∂_i ∂_j (1/|r + u|) ],                                    (5)

and for `r > D`, which already rules out overlap, expanding in `u` and averaging term by term gives

    N_ij(r) = −(V/4π) Σ_{m ≥ 0} T_(2m+3)(r̂) / r^(2m+3),                            (6)

where `T_(2m+3)` is a polynomial in the direction cosines whose coefficients are polynomials in the squared edges. `demag-scripts/series_terms.py` computes these terms to any order in exact rational arithmetic. The series is not new. Donahue gives it to all orders in operator form [Donahue2007], and OOMMF's `demagcoef.cc` carries its first four terms, `T_3` to `T_9`; our exact derivation reproduces OOMMF's integer coefficients for `Nxx` and `Nxy`. Write `S_n` for (6) truncated after the terms through `1/r^(n−2)`, so that the omitted terms start at `1/r^n`. OOMMF's asymptotic order is this `n`. `S_n` is the exact truncated series; (7) and (8) below bound its truncation error only, and a floating-point evaluation adds rounding error.

**Theorem 8.1.** For all edges `(Δx, Δy, Δz)`, every component `(i, j)`, every odd `n ≥ 3` and every `r > D`,

    |N_ij(r) − S_n(r)| ≤ V D^(n−3) / ( 2π r^(n−2) (r² − D²) ),                      (7)

and, using the cell shape,

    |N_ij(r) − S_n(r)| ≤ (V/4π) [ (n−2)(n−1) E|u|^(n−3) / r^n + 2 D^(n−1) / ( r^n (r² − D²) ) ],   (8)

whose right-hand side never exceeds that of (7). The constant in (7) is attained, for the diagonal component along the axis, in the limit of a thin needle with offset along its axis, so it cannot be lowered without using the cell shape.

*Proof.* Fix `u`; then `|u| ≤ D < r`. Write `φ = ∂_i ∂_j (1/|x|)`, and let `e_i`, `e_j` be the coordinate unit vectors. The function `t ↦ φ(r + t u)` extends holomorphically to the disc `|t| < r/|u|`, whose radius exceeds 1, because the zeros of the quadratic `|r + t u|²` in `t` have modulus `r/|u|`; so its Taylor series in `t` converges at `t = 1`. Its `t^k` coefficient is the `k`-th term of the Taylor series of `φ` about `r`, evaluated at `r + u`: `(1/k!) ∇^(k+2)(1/|x|)(r)[u, …, u, e_i, e_j]`. The `(k+2)`-th derivative of `1/|x|` is a symmetric multilinear form on Euclidean space, and the norm of such a form equals its largest absolute value on the diagonal, over unit vectors [Banach1938, Satz I; in finite dimensions it also follows, by induction on the degree, from the gradient bound of Kellogg1928, Theorem IV]. For a unit vector `v` at angle `γ` to `x`, the Legendre generating function gives `(v·∇)^ℓ (1/|x|) = (−1)^ℓ ℓ! P_ℓ(cos γ) / |x|^(ℓ+1)`, where `P_ℓ` is the Legendre polynomial of degree `ℓ`, and `|P_ℓ| ≤ 1` with equality at `γ = 0`. The norm of the `ℓ`-th derivative at `r` is therefore exactly `ℓ!/r^(ℓ+1)`; with `ℓ = k + 2`, the `k`-th term is at most `(k+1)(k+2) |u|^k / r^(k+3)`. These bounds form a convergent series, so the expansion may be averaged term by term. The odd terms average to zero because `u` and `−u` have the same law. For even `k = 2s`, `E|u|^k` is the multinomial sum of `E[u_x^(2a)] E[u_y^(2b)] E[u_z^(2c)]` over `a + b + c = s`. One has `μ_a μ_b ≤ μ_(a+b)`: putting `A = 2a+1` and `B = 2b+1`, the difference `A(A+1)B(B+1) − 2(A+B−1)(A+B)` is a quadratic in `A` with nonnegative leading coefficient `B(B+1) − 2`, it vanishes at `A = 1`, and its derivative there is `(3B+2)(B−1) ≥ 0`. Hence `E|u|^(2s) ≤ μ_s D^(2s)`, that is `(k+1)(k+2) E|u|^k ≤ 2 D^k`, with equality for a needle. The omitted terms of `S_n` start at `k = n − 3`, so `|N_ij − S_n| ≤ (V/4π) Σ 2 D^k / r^(k+3)` over even `k ≥ n − 3`, a geometric series in `D²/r²` whose sum is (7). Keeping the exact moment for `k = n − 3` gives (8). In the limit of a needle of vanishing width, with offset and component along its axis, every inequality above becomes an equality; after division by `V` both sides of (7) depend continuously on the edges, so the ratio of error to bound tends to 1, which gives the last statement. ∎

**Tests.** `demag-scripts/check_remainder_bound.py` tests (7) and (8) at `n = 5, 7, 9, 11` against every certified entry with `r > D` in both certificates (1,162 entries of `demag_certificate_six_cells.json`, which include the 776 of `demag_certificate.json`), in exact rational arithmetic, with `π` and `r` bracketed on the safe side and the true value anywhere in its certified enclosure. No comparison fails. The largest ratio of error to bound is 0.833, for (7) at `n = 5`, the elongated cell along its axis at 10,000 cells. The script also checks the moment inequality for every cell. These checks test Theorem 8.1; the proof is above. Separately, a test with the private interval engine, which is not replayable from this repository, covered 2,759 random cases (all six components, cell aspect ratios up to about 1,000, `r/D` from 1.0001 to 3,150) and found no violation of (7) or (8); the largest ratio was 0.99997, for a 1 : 1/300 : 1/300 needle along its axis. The second certificate, `demag_certificate_six_cells.json`, extends the first by two thin-film cells, `(1, 1, 1/10)` and `(1, 1.3, 1/10)`; its 862 entries for the original four cells are identical to those of the first, and it passes `check_demag.py` unchanged.

**Relation to OOMMF's switching radii.** Since version 2.0a2, OOMMF's `FindKnotSchedule` chooses its switching radii from assumed error and speed models [OOMMF; DP2019]. The one for the base, unsubdivided asymptotic method is stated in a source comment, in our notation, as `V hmax^(n−3) / (5 r^(n−2) (r² − hmax²))`, with `hmax` the longest edge. Bound (7) has the same form, with `D` in place of `hmax` and `1/(2π)` in place of `1/5`. It is loose for compact cells. For a cube at `n = 11`, in units of `(V/4π) hmax^8 / r^11`, the leading error coefficient is `99/64` (`Nxx` with offset along `x`, or any diagonal component along its own axis; the largest over directions and components); OOMMF's model gives `4π/5 ≈ 2.5`, bound (7) gives `162` and bound (8) gives `1122/35`, about 105 and 21 times the true coefficient.

**Prior work.** The series is Donahue's [Donahue2007], and its first four terms are in OOMMF. The argument above is the three-dimensional form of the classical multipole truncation estimate, which [GR1987] gives in two dimensions, applied to the second derivatives of the kernel averaged over the pair of cells. Buchheit, Busse, Keßler and Rybakov give a rigorous remainder estimate for the cuboid moment series by Cauchy estimates [BBKR2026, Corollary 2.5]; its constant is not explicit and it requires a separation larger than a multiple of the cell size. A search on 2026-11-10 (record: `SWEEP-RECORD-DEMAG-BOUND-2026-11-10.md`) found no bound of the explicit form (7). The search does not rule out a bound of this form elsewhere.
